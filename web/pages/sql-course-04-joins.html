Lesson 4: Join on a relationship

Assume customers(customer_id, name) and orders(order_id, customer_id, total). Qualify columns with aliases whenever joined tables share column names.

SELECT c.customer_id, c.name, o.order_id, o.total
FROM customers AS c
INNER JOIN orders AS o ON o.customer_id = c.customer_id;

Protect the row count

A join can multiply rows when a key matches multiple records. Check uniqueness on both sides before assuming one-to-one output. Put right-side filters in the ON clause when unmatched left rows must remain; placing them in WHERE can turn a LEFT JOIN into an effective INNER JOIN.
